# Valley View sky highlights — nights of Sept 9 & 10, 2026

*A short, easy tour. For the deep version, see the full
[Astronomy Evening at Valley View](../astro-evening-vv.md) tour.*

---

## Why these two nights are special

**No Moon.** New Moon falls on Sept 10, so on both nights the Moon is gone
before the sky is fully dark — under Valley View's
[Bortle 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bortle_scale) skies that's about as
dark as it ever gets.

**When it gets dark (MDT):**

| | Sunset | Full dark | Dark ends (a.m.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tue Sep 9  | 7:20 | 8:50 | 5:10 |
| Wed Sep 10 | 7:18 | 8:48 | 5:11 |

The sky keeps getting better through the evening as the summer Milky Way
swings higher.

---

## Naked eye — start here

- **The Milky Way, horizon to horizon.** Once it's dark it arches across the
  whole sky: the bright star clouds of **Sagittarius** low in the southwest
  (you're looking toward the center of our galaxy), straight up through
  **Cygnus** overhead, and down into **Cassiopeia** in the northeast. Look for
  the **Great Rift** — the dark lane of dust splitting the glow in two. On a
  night this dark it can be bright enough to cast a faint shadow.

- **The Summer Triangle**, nearly overhead: **Vega, Deneb, Altair**. Same
  little triangle, wildly different distances — Altair 17 light-years, Vega 25,
  Deneb over 1,500.

- **Andromeda Galaxy (M31)** clears the eastern ridge by mid-evening — the
  farthest thing visible to the unaided eye, 2.5 million light-years away, and
  several times wider than the full Moon.

- **Venus**, brilliant but very low in the west — it sets less than 1½ hours
  after the Sun. Catch it in binoculars right at dusk or not at all.

---

## In the telescope

- **Ring Nebula (M57)** — almost straight up all evening. A blue-green smoke
  ring: the puffed-off outer layers of a dying star.

- **Albireo** — the classic double star in Cygnus, a gold star and a blue one
  side by side.

- **M13** — the great globular cluster in Hercules, high in the west; a few
  hundred thousand stars in one ball.

- **Saturn** rises in the southeast around 8:30 p.m. and is well up by
  10–10:30. The rings are nearly edge-on right now — a thin bright line — plus
  its big moon **Titan**. Faint blue **Neptune** sits about a fist-width away
  in Pisces for anyone who wants one more planet in the eyepiece.
