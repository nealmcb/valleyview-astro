## Tonight's sky

**No Moon.** A 1%-lit crescent sets before sunset, so it's dark all night —
under Valley View's [Bortle 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bortle_scale)
skies, about as good as it gets. New Moon is tomorrow.

**Times (MDT):** sunset **7:20**, fully dark **8:50 p.m.**, first light **5:10 a.m.**
The sky keeps improving through the evening as the summer
[Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way) climbs higher.

## Naked eye — start here

- **The Milky Way, horizon to horizon.** Once it's dark it arches across the
  whole sky: the bright star clouds of
  [Sagittarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_(constellation)) low
  in the southwest (toward the
  [center of our galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center)),
  straight up through
  [Cygnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_(constellation)) overhead, and
  down into
  [Cassiopeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiopeia_(constellation)) in the
  northeast. Look for the
  [**Great Rift**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_(astronomy)) — the
  dark dust lane splitting the glow. On a night this dark it can be bright
  enough to cast a faint shadow.

- **The [Summer Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Triangle)**,
  nearly overhead: [Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega),
  [Deneb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deneb), and
  [Altair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altair). One big triangle, three
  wildly different distances — Altair 17 light-years, Vega 25, Deneb over 1,500.

- **[Andromeda Galaxy (M31)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andromeda_Galaxy)**
  clears the mountains to the east-northeast about **7:50 p.m.** To the naked
  eye it's a faint grey smudge — but that smudge is a whole other galaxy, 2.5
  million light-years away, and the farthest thing you can see without a
  telescope. A long exposure through a good scope shows it spanning **about six
  times the width of the full Moon** (roughly 3°), fainter outer arms reaching
  further still.

- **[Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus)**, brilliant but very low in
  the west — it sets less than 1½ hours after the Sun. Binoculars right at dusk
  or not at all.

## Satellites

- **The Space Station
  ([ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station))** —
  a brilliant pass at **8:03 p.m.**, magnitude −3.8 (brighter than anything
  else in the sky): up from the southwest, 66° high in the southeast, out in
  the northeast. A second, lower, dimmer pass at 9:41.
- **[Tiangong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_space_station)**,
  China's space station — **8:54 p.m.**, magnitude −2.2, from the
  west-northwest to 74° up in the south.
- Plus spent rocket bodies (the tumbling *SL‑16* Zenit stages sometimes flare)
  and reconnaissance satellites at 2nd–3rd magnitude.

## In the telescope

Mostly the 20" [PlaneWave](../telescopes.md):

- **[Ring Nebula (M57)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_Nebula)** — nearly
  straight up all evening. A blue-green smoke ring: the puffed-off outer layers
  of a dying star.
- **[Albireo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albireo)** — the classic double
  star in Cygnus, a gold star and a blue one side by side.
- **[M13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messier_13)** — the great globular
  cluster in [Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules_(constellation)),
  high in the west; a few hundred thousand stars in one ball.
- **[Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn)** clears the ridge around
  9:45 p.m. and is well up in the southeast by 11. Rings nearly edge-on right
  now — a thin bright line — plus its moon
  [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_(moon)). Faint blue
  [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune) sits about a fist-width away
  in [Pisces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisces_(constellation)).
