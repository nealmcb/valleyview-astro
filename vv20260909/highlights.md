# Valley View sky highlights — nights of Sept 9 & 10, 2026

*A short, easy tour. For the deep version, see the full

## Why these two nights are special

**No Moon.** New Moon falls on Sept 10, so on both nights the Moon is gone
before the sky is fully dark — under Valley View's
[Bortle 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bortle_scale) skies that's about as
dark as it ever gets.

**When it gets dark (MDT):**

| | Sunset | Full dark | Dark ends (a.m.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tue Sep 9  | 7:20 | 8:50 | 5:10 |
| Wed Sep 10 | 7:18 | 8:48 | 5:11 |

The sky keeps getting better through the evening as the summer
[Milky Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milky_Way) swings higher.

## Naked eye — start here

- **The Milky Way, horizon to horizon.** Once it's dark it arches across the
  whole sky: the bright star clouds of
  [Sagittarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_(constellation)) low
  in the southwest (you're looking toward the
  [center of our galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Center)),
  straight up through
  [Cygnus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cygnus_(constellation)) overhead, and
  down into
  [Cassiopeia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiopeia_(constellation)) in the
  northeast. Look for the
  [**Great Rift**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Rift_(astronomy)) — the
  dark lane of dust splitting the glow in two. On a night this dark it can be
  bright enough to cast a faint shadow.

- **The [Summer Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Triangle)**,
  nearly overhead: [Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega),
  [Deneb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deneb), and
  [Altair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altair). Same little triangle, wildly
  different distances — Altair 17 light-years, Vega 25, Deneb over 1,500.

- **[Andromeda Galaxy (M31)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andromeda_Galaxy)**
  clears the mountains to the east-northeast about **7:50 p.m.** and is well up
  by the time the sky is fully dark. It's the farthest thing visible to the
  unaided eye — 2.5 million light-years — and several times wider than the full
  Moon.

- **[Venus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus)**, brilliant but very low in
  the west — it sets less than 1½ hours after the Sun. Catch it in binoculars
  right at dusk or not at all.

## Satellites passing over

Something bright drifts through most clear nights here. The pick of them on
**Tue Sept 9** (times MDT):

- **The Space Station ([ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Space_Station))**
  — a brilliant pass at **8:03 p.m.**, magnitude −3.8 (brighter than anything
  else in the sky): up from the southwest, 66° high in the southeast, fading
  out in the northeast. It swings back lower and dimmer at 9:41.
- **[Tiangong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_space_station)**, China's
  space station — **8:54 p.m.**, magnitude −2.2, from the west-northwest to 74°
  up in the south.
- In between, a steady trickle of spent rocket bodies (the tumbling *SL‑16*
  Zenit upper stages sometimes flare) and reconnaissance satellites at 2nd–3rd
  magnitude — see the table below.

Full predictions for any night: [heavens-above.com](https://www.heavens-above.com/).

## In the telescope

- **[Ring Nebula (M57)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_Nebula)** — almost
  straight up all evening. A blue-green smoke ring: the puffed-off outer layers
  of a dying star.

- **[Albireo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albireo)** — the classic double
  star in Cygnus, a gold star and a blue one side by side.

- **[M13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messier_13)** — the great globular
  cluster in [Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hercules_(constellation)),
  high in the west; a few hundred thousand stars in one ball.

- **[Saturn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturn)** clears the ridge around
  9:45 p.m. and is well up in the southeast by 11. The rings are nearly
  edge-on right now — a thin bright line — plus its big moon
  [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_(moon)). Faint blue
  [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune) sits about a fist-width away
  in [Pisces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisces_(constellation)) for anyone
  who wants one more planet in the eyepiece.
