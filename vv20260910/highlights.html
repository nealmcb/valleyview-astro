Tonight’s sky

The darkest night of the month. New Moon is tonight (exactly 9:27 p.m.), so there is no Moon at all — under Valley View’s Bortle 2 skies, about as dark as the sky ever gets.

Times (MDT): sunset 7:18, fully dark 8:48 p.m., first light 5:11 a.m. The sky keeps improving through the evening as the summer Milky Way climbs higher.

Naked eye — start here

  • The Milky Way, horizon to horizon. Once it’s dark it arches across the whole sky: the bright star clouds of Sagittarius low in the southwest (toward the center of our galaxy), straight up through Cygnus overhead, and down into Cassiopeia in the northeast. Look for the Great Rift — the dark dust lane splitting the glow. On a night this dark it can be bright enough to cast a faint shadow.

  • The Summer Triangle, nearly overhead: Vega, Deneb, and Altair. Same little triangle, wildly different distances — Altair 17 light-years, Vega 25, Deneb over 1,500.

  • Andromeda Galaxy (M31) clears the mountains to the east-northeast about 7:45 p.m. To the naked eye it’s a faint grey smudge — but that smudge is a whole other galaxy, 2.5 million light-years away, and the farthest thing you can see without a telescope. A long exposure through a good scope shows it spanning about six times the width of the full Moon (roughly 3°), fainter outer arms reaching further still.

  • Venus, brilliant but very low in the west — it sets less than 1½ hours after the Sun. Binoculars right at dusk or not at all.

Satellites

  • Tiangong, China’s space station — the night’s best pass, right at dusk at 7:56 p.m., magnitude −2.1, from the west-northwest almost overhead toward the north. A second, low pass at 9:33.
  • The Space Station (ISS) manages only a low, modest pass at 8:53 p.m. (magnitude −1.6, 26° up in the north).
  • Hubble scrapes the southern horizon around 8:59 p.m.
  • Plus spent rocket bodies (the tumbling SL‑16 Zenit stages sometimes flare) at 2nd–3rd magnitude.

In the telescope

Mostly the 20" PlaneWave:

  • Ring Nebula (M57) — nearly straight up all evening. A blue-green smoke ring: the puffed-off outer layers of a dying star.
  • Albireo — the classic double star in Cygnus, a gold star and a blue one side by side.
  • M13 — the great globular cluster in Hercules, high in the west; a few hundred thousand stars in one ball.
  • Saturn clears the ridge around 9:45 p.m. and is well up in the southeast by 11. Rings nearly edge-on right now — a thin bright line — plus its moon Titan. Faint blue Neptune sits about a fist-width away in Pisces.
