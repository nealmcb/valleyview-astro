# 🌌 Astronomy Evening at Valley View Hot Springs  
*Tour of the 3D Universe far from light pollution with eyes, binocs and an amazing 20" PlaneWave Telescope*  

---

We often dream of travelling the world and seeing its many wonders,
but few people have the time or money to see much of it.  Even though
the distances are much greater, astronomy is more accessible than
geography: half the universe is visible on any clear evening, and you
can look at most of it in one night.

The universe is easy to observe, dynamic, deeply related to our
existance (the hopfully-familiar story of the big bang and creation
and supernovae creating the elements that our bodies are made of.)

See the Moon, constellations, stars, planets, Earth satellites,
and more via the naked eye, binoculars and a small telescope.
For your mind's eye we'll point out newly-discovered solar systems,
black holes, X-ray sources, the Hubble Deep Field and bask (as always
:-) in the presence of the 2.73 degree K cosmic microwave background
radiation from the Big Bang.

## 1. **Setting the Scene: Valley View’s Dark Sky**
- **Dark site:** [Bortle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bortle_scale) 2 skies.
  - Compare to nearby Great Sand Dunes NP.
  - Naked-eye phenomena possible here:
    - **Zodiacal light** (cone after dusk/dawn)
    - **Gegenschein** (opposite the Sun at midnight)
    - **Zodiacal band** stretching across ecliptic
    - **Airglow** (green/red natural skyglow from upper atmosphere)
	- **Clouds look black** not lit from below
    - **Dark nebulae** in the Milky Way
    - **Milky Way shadows** on the ground

## 2. **Our Telescope: Beyond Magic**
- **20" PlaneWave telescope + L-500 mount + CCD imaging + computer.**
- **Why remarkable:**
  - Would have sped up worldwide science immeasurably even 50 years ago.
  - Beyond *magic* 100 years ago.
- **Detector progress:**
  - Photographic plates: QE ~1%-3%
  - CCDs: QE ~10% (1980s)
  - Modern CMOS/CCD: QE ~90%
  - → ~100× more efficient in half a century.
- **What this system can do:**
  - Detect **exoplanet transits**.
  - Invaluable for **variable star tracking**.
  - Reveal faint **galaxies, nebulae, and streams**.
- **Historical aside:** Laurent Cassegrain’s reflecting telescope design was ridiculed by Newton and Huygens, suppressing it for centuries. His full identity was forgotten until rediscovered in 2000. Tonight’s telescope vindicates him.

## 3. **How the Naked Eye Misleads Us**
1. **Our cosmos is filled with galaxies.**
   - Yet only Andromeda (M31, ~2.5 Mly) is obvious to the eye.
2. **Light pollution is erasing stars.**
   - Star visibility drops ~9–10% globally each year.
3. **Stars move.**
   - Barnard’s Star and 61 Cygni show rapid proper motion, but imperceptible to the human eye in one lifetime.
4. **Bright stars are unusual.**
   - Most stars (~75–80%) are faint red dwarfs.
   - None visible to naked eye.
   - Our Sun is brighter than 95% of stars, but dwarfed by O-type-stars millions of times more luminous.
5. **Stars are often in systems.**
   - ~50% in doubles or multiples.
   - Castor in Gemini = 6 stars.
   - TIC 168789840 = 6 eclipsing stars, all interacting.

## 4. **Dynamic, 3D Sky**
- **Summer Triangle:**
  - Altair: 17 ly
  - Vega: 25 ly
  - Deneb: 1,500–2,600 ly (uncertain; Gaia saturates on such bright stars)
  - → same sky triangle, immense depth difference.
- **Sagittarius stream:**
  - Stars from the Sagittarius Dwarf Galaxy, which is being torn apart by the milky way.
- **Rubin Observatory (LSST):**
  - Will catalog more galaxies than stars - tens of billions.
  - Will produce a *movie* of the sky from Chile - the whole southern sky every 3 days. Will alert us to about 10 million changes in the sky every night, all public.
  - M49 in the [Virgo Cluster](https://rubinobservatory.org/gallery/collections/first-look-gallery/qe2do9iu6h1gbdg4bsgc1o083u) is nearly as far across as the moon

## 6. **Cosmic Highlights**
- **M31 Andromeda Galaxy:**
  - Distance: ~2.5 Mly.
  - Angular size: ~3° across (about 6 full Moons); fainter outer disk traces to ~5° in deep exposures.
- **3C273 (brightest quasar):** near Eta Virgo
  - Distance: ~2.4 Gly (z=0.158).
  - Varies between mag 12.5–13.5. both daily and yearly.
- **Einstein rings:**
  - Galaxy acting like a funhouse mirror, bending light from something billions of light-years farther away.
  - We see multiple copies of the same object, or a perfect ring if the aligment is perfect.
  - Einstein Cross (Q2237+0305) in Pegasus.
- **Cygnus X-1:**
  - Distance: 7,260 ly.
  - Black hole mass: ~21 M☉, near-maximal spin.
  - First confirmed stellar-mass black hole.
- **[Antlia Supernova Remnant](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/522219/fulltext/21728.text.html#fg1)** above the horizon around February. Dec -36. 200-1000 ly distant, perhaps 100,000 years ago.  May be one of the closest supernova remnants to Earth, second only to the Local Bubble.  centered on (l = 276.52&j0;, b = +19.05&j0;) == PSR B0950+08. would have been as bright as the moon. 
- **Betelgeuse:**
  - Will explode as supernova (sometime in next ~100k yrs).
  - Current variability: 0.0–1.3 mag over ~423 days.
- **Nearby supernova candidate:** IK Pegasi, ~150 ly. Type Ia progenitor.

## 7. **Largest Structures**
- **Hercules–Corona Borealis Great Wall:**
  - ~10 billion light-years across.
  - Detected via clustering of GRBs.
  - Highly controversial; may contradict cosmological principle (homogeneity at >300 Mpc).
- Other debated “walls” and “rings” (e.g. Sloan Great Wall).
- Likely selection effects—worth presenting as *tentative, under debate*.

## 9. **Closing Themes**
- **Dynamic sky:** stars move, vary, explode.
- Half of Earth’s internal heat flow comes from radioactive decay; the rest is “primordial heat” left over from planet formation. So when you soak, you are literally bathing in the warmth of Earth’s nuclear heart.
- **We are stardust:** every atom of our bodies forged in ancient stars.
- So while you soak here in the warm springs, remember — the hydrogen in the water of your cells was born in the Big Bang, the calcium in your bones was forged in dying stars. The gold in a ring and the uranium keeping the earth hot both needed cataclysms far beyond Earth: supernova explosions, and collisions of neutron stars. You’re surrounded by atoms forged in the most violent places in the cosmos. Valley View isn’t just connected to the Earth — it’s connected to the whole universe.
- The heat beneath your feet: Half of Earth’s warmth comes from slow radioactive decay of uranium, thorium, and potassium, the rest from the planet’s fiery birth. The springs here are literally powered by Earth’s nuclear furnace.
- The atoms within you: Your body is made of stardust — oxygen and carbon from stars, calcium and iron from dying suns, trace metals from the most violent explosions in the universe.
- The story that connects it all: The gold in a wedding band, the uranium that keeps the crust hot, even the water in your cells — all came from cataclysms beyond Earth: supernovae and neutron-star collisions. Tonight at Valley View, you’re soaking not only in warm mineral water, but in the story of the universe itself.
